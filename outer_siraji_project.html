The Outer Siraji Language Project

Preserving the unique voice of the Inner Himalayas

What is Outer Siraji?

Outer Siraji is a unique Western Pahari language spoken in the Anni and Nirmand regions of Kullu, Himachal Pradesh. For centuries, its identity has been a puzzle, caught between the major languages of Kullu and Shimla and often confused with a similarly named language in Jammu & Kashmir.

This project is dedicated to documenting Outer Siraji, a language classified as definitely endangered. Our goal is to create a permanent record of its unique grammar, rich vocabulary, and the priceless oral traditions of its speakers before they fade away.

By preserving this language, we save more than words; we save a unique worldview shaped by the Himalayan landscape.

The heartland of Outer Siraji: the Anni and Nirmand tehsils, nestled between the Kullu and Shimla regions.

A Language on a Bridge

Outer Siraji is not just a dialect; it's a linguistic bridge. Its features show connections to two major Pahari language groups, making it a fascinating subject for study.

Affiliation: Mahasu Pahari

Most linguists, and databases like Glottolog, classify Outer Siraji as a dialect of Mahasu Pahari, the language of the neighboring Shimla region. This is supported by geography and shared cultural ties.

A Rare Grammatical Treasure

Outer Siraji has a Verb-Second (V2) word order, similar to German or Kashmiri. The verb must be the second element in a sentence. This is extremely rare among Indo-Aryan languages and makes its preservation scientifically vital.

Connection: Kullui

Despite its Mahasui classification, it is spoken in Kullu district and speakers can generally understand Kullui. This shows the deep and continuous nature of language in the Himalayas.

Research & Resources

Our understanding of Outer Siraji is built on a handful of key sources, but there is a critical gap.

The Dictionary Gap

There is no published dictionary for Outer Siraji. Creating the first-ever Outer Siraji-Hindi-English dictionary is the primary goal of this preservation effort. The vocabulary from the folktales in our archive is the first step in this monumental task.

Key Academic Sources:

  • Grierson's *Linguistic Survey of India* (Vol. IX, Part IV) - The foundational, historical survey.
  • T. Grahame Bailey's *Languages of the Northern Himalayas* (1908) - Crucial early comparative work.
  • J.C. Sharma's "Outer Siraji: An SVO language" (2003) - The most important modern paper identifying the V2 grammar.
  • Central Institute of Indian Languages (CIIL) - Potential project materials from the early 2000s.
